# Voices

How each person in Saksham talks (BUILD_PLAN P6, D-052). Read this before writing or changing any line.

**Rules for every line**

- Short: 18 words or fewer for a spoken line (the validator checks it), 22 for a choice. Split a long thought into two lines.
- Natural spoken Indian English: "Amma", "Didi", "ji", "Arre", "Namaste". Contractions are fine. No slang a teacher would wince at.
- Specific details beat general ones: "six in the morning", "a Class 6 maths notebook", "the new hand pump".
- Consequences feel real but are never graphic. The child is the helper or a witness, never the one harmed on screen.
- The legal text on a card never changes here. When someone explains a right in dialogue, they say the same thing the card says, in plain words.

## Mitthu (the parrot guide)

Warm, curious and a little funny, never preachy. Mitthu notices things, wonders out loud, and nudges the player towards a grown-up who can help. He never lectures and never tells the player they were wrong: a weaker choice gets "Let's try that again." He makes the odd joke about being a parrot, but never during a sad moment.

- "Welcome to the town! Walk down the street and peek into any open door."
- "The playground swings are being fixed. I've asked for a parrot-sized one. Coming soon!"
- "Hmm. Is that really true? Let's read the notice board, then ask someone who knows."

## The player

Kind and direct. The player says what they saw, asks simple questions, and offers to help. Never bossy, never a hero speech.

- "Why don't you come in with us?"
- "You don't look fine. You can tell me, if you want."
- "Can I tell Pooja she isn't alone?"

## Ravi (School, about 10)

Friendly and chatty, the first kid you meet. He notices Lata before anyone else does and is a bit in awe of her maths.

- "Hi! See that girl by the gate? That's Lata."
- "She comes here every morning and watches us go in. But she never comes inside."
- "She's shy, but really nice. And she does sums in her head faster than me!"

## Lata (School, 9)

Shy and quiet, then proud. She repeats what grown-ups told her because she believes it.

- "Oh! Hello. I'm Lata. I like watching the morning assembly."
- "Amma says school costs money. We don't have any extra."
- "Meena Didi gave me a new notebook. And guess what? I'm the fastest at sums in class!"

## Meena Didi (School teacher)

Calm, busy and practical. She thanks the child first, explains the right in one clear sentence, then says exactly what she will do today.

- "Good morning! I'm getting the classroom ready."
- "Thank you for telling me. You did exactly the right thing."
- "I'll take the admission form to her Amma today. Will you come and introduce me?"

## Raju (Dhaba, 12)

Polite, tired and in a hurry. He doesn't complain; he misses school in small, specific ways.

- "Oh, hello. I'm Raju. Sorry, I can't stop. There are lots of glasses to wash."
- "I miss my friends. And maths. I was good at maths."
- "Thanks for talking to me. Most people just order chai."

## Mohan Bhaiya (Dhaba owner)

Cheerful and rushed, a busy shopkeeper. Not a villain: he just hasn't thought about it.

- "Welcome, welcome! Chai in two minutes."
- "Sorry, it's very busy this morning."
- "Two cutting chai for table three!"

## Sunita Didi (anganwadi worker)

Warm and steady, knows every family near the well. She explains, then makes a plan that helps the family as well as the child.

- "Hello! I'm Sunita, from the anganwadi by the well."
- "Faster? He's only a child. Tell me, why is he working at all?"
- "I'll visit his family today. There's help for families when a parent is ill."

## Kabir (Home, about 10)

Sporty and blunt, the friend who says what everyone has noticed.

- "Want to play cricket?"
- "Anu used to bowl for us every evening."
- "Now she just stands by the stairs. I think something's wrong."

## Anu (Home, about 10)

Quiet, careful, and relieved once she has said it. Her words are simple; the scene never shows what happened.

- "Oh, hi. I'm fine. I just don't want to go upstairs yet."
- "He says it's a family secret and I mustn't tell anyone."
- "Thank you for listening. I feel a little better already."

## Farida Aunty (Home, health worker)

Gentle and unhurried. She makes it clear, more than once, that telling was right and that it is never the child's fault.

- "Hello! Back from school? I'm just watering my plants before my shift."
- "Telling was the right thing to do, even though it was a secret."
- "If Anu isn't safe at home, we'll call 1098 together."

## Sonu (Panchayat, Pooja's younger brother)

Worried about his sister and a bit embarrassed to say so.

- "That's my didi, Pooja, under the tree."
- "She always helps me with homework."
- "But this week she keeps crying when she thinks nobody's looking."

## Pooja (Panchayat, 15)

Studious and hopeful, with a clear dream, unsure how to stand up to her family.

- "Sorry, I'm trying to study. My board exams start next month."
- "But I want to finish school and become a nurse."
- "It helped to say it out loud. Thank you for listening."

## Sarpanch Kavita ji (Panchayat)

Respected and plain-spoken. She runs meetings about hand pumps and weddings alike, states the law simply, and acts the same day.

- "Namaste! The meeting starts soon. We're deciding where the new hand pump should go."
- "In India, a girl must be at least eighteen to marry, and a boy at least twenty-one."
- "I'll visit Pooja's parents this evening, with the Child Marriage Prohibition Officer."
